# Reference data sources

## oral_cancer/ (Oral Cancer & Tobacco page)

Every file in `oral_cancer/` is generated by a script in `ingestion/`
that downloads its source and parses it, refusing to write partial
output. `python3 -m scripts.build_oral_cancer_data` rebuilds them all;
two consecutive rebuilds produce byte-identical files.
`oral_cancer/SOURCES.csv` is the source table shown on the page.

| File | Script | Source | Granularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nfhs5_state_tobacco.csv` | `nfhs5_report_tables` | NFHS-5 India Report Table 2.36 | India + 36 states x sex x urban/rural |
| `nfhs5_tobacco_by_background.csv` | `nfhs5_report_tables` | Table 2.35 | India x sex x urban/rural x age, schooling, religion, caste, wealth |
| `nfhs5_tobacco_products.csv` | `nfhs5_report_tables` | Table 11.7 | India x sex x urban/rural x product (age 15-49) |
| `nfhs5_quitting_by_state.csv` | `nfhs5_report_tables` | Table 11.10 | India + 36 states x sex |
| `nfhs6_state_tobacco.csv` | `nfhs6_factsheets` | NFHS-6 (2023-24) fact sheets, IIPS May 2026 | India + 35 states (no Manipur) x sex x urban/rural |
| `nfhs5_state_screening.csv` | `nfhs5_factsheets` | NFHS-5 state fact sheets (pratapvardhan/NFHS-5 extraction) | India + 36 states |
| `nfhs5_district_indicators.csv` | `nfhs5_factsheets` | NFHS-5 district fact sheets (3 open extractions, cross-validated) | 707 districts |
| `gats2_tobacco_type_by_state.csv` | `gats2` | GATS-2 India Report Table 4.6 | India + 32 states |
| `ncrp_oral_cancer_projections.csv` | `ncrp2020` | NCRP 2020 Table 14.1 | India x sex x year (tongue, mouth) |
| `ncrp_mouth_cancer_stage.csv` | `ncrp2020` | NCRP 2020 Table 9.2.1 | India x sex x stage |
| `district_population_2020.csv` | `district_population` | WorldPop 2020 1 km grid (UN-adjusted), summed over NFHS-5 district boundaries | 707 districts |
| `state_population_2011_current.csv` | `state_population` | `state_population_2011.csv`, split to current units | 36 states |
| `../../geo/nfhs5_states.geojson`, `nfhs5_districts.geojson` | `nfhs5_boundaries` | DHS Spatial Data Repository, survey IA2020DHS | 36 states, 707 districts |

**Cross-checks run on every rebuild.**
- NFHS-5 state tobacco from Table 2.36 matches the NFHS-5 column in
  the NFHS-6 fact sheets (within 0.3 pp, all 35 shared states) and the
  state fact-sheet extraction (within 0.5 pp, all 36).
- GATS-2 product types sum to "current user" in every row. NCRP men +
  women equal "both sexes" in every column.
- District extractions: see the `ingestion/nfhs5_factsheets.py`
  docstring. One corrupt row (Saiha) is patched, and 9 disputed
  districts are flagged and left uncoloured on maps.

**Confidence notes.**
- The district and state fact-sheet values come from open
  third-party extractions, because the official PDFs (rchiips.org)
  are offline. They agree with each other and with the NFHS-5 report
  where they overlap, but they are not read from the originals.
- The model's relative risks are literature values found via search
  abstracts, not re-read from the full papers: smokeless tobacco 4.44
  (Asthana et al. 2019, *Nicotine & Tobacco Research* 21(9):1162-71,
  SEAR) and bidi smoking 2.6 (pooled random-effects OR across 10 Indian
  case-control studies, Rahman et al. 2005, *Preventive Medicine*).
  Both are adjustable on the page.
- Three hosts (nfhsiips.in, ntcp.mohfw.gov.in, ncdirindia.org) serve
  incomplete TLS certificate chains, so those public PDFs are
  downloaded without certificate verification.

## Older files (HPV page and the earlier oral cancer projection)

These files back the HPV simulation page. They are
external reference data, not part of the main dataset catalogue
(`data/india_data.db`) -- kept as separate flat files since they're a
small, fixed set of figures, not something `scripts/ingest.py`
fetches or refreshes.

## state_population_2011.csv

2011 Census of India official state/UT population totals, on the
**undivided** pre-reorganization boundaries (pre-2014 Andhra
Pradesh/Telangana split, pre-2019 Jammu & Kashmir/Ladakh split,
pre-2020 Daman & Diu / Dadra & Nagar Haveli merger) -- matching the
state boundaries in `data/geo/india_states.geojson`.

Source: Registrar General & Census Commissioner of India, 2011
Census. Cross-checked via public tabulations (Wikipedia's Census
2011 summary table) for the modern split figures, with undivided
totals for AP, J&K, and Daman & Diu / DNH obtained from combining
their post-split successor units' individually reported 2011 figures.

## nfhs5_tobacco_use_by_state_raw.csv and nfhs5_tobacco_use_by_state.csv

Percentage of the population age 15+ currently using any tobacco
product, by state/UT and sex.

Source: National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019-21, India
Report, Table 2.36 ("Use of tobacco by the population age 15 and
over by state/union territory"). Ministry of Health & Family
Welfare, Government of India / International Institute for
Population Sciences. Full report:
https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/FR375/FR375.pdf.

**Generated by `ingestion/nfhs5_tobacco.py`**, not hand-typed: the
script downloads that PDF directly, locates Table 2.36 with `pypdf`,
and parses each state's row out of the extracted text. Run
`python3 -m ingestion.nfhs5_tobacco` to regenerate both files from a
fresh download at any time.

- `nfhs5_tobacco_use_by_state_raw.csv`: NFHS-5's own current state
  boundaries, exactly as published (36 rows).
- `nfhs5_tobacco_use_by_state.csv`: the one actually used by the
  simulation -- `_raw`'s Andhra Pradesh/Telangana and J&K/Ladakh rows
  population-weighted back into undivided totals (35 rows), to match
  `data/geo/india_states.geojson`'s 2011-era geometry. Daman and Diu
  / Dadra and Nagar Haveli both carry NFHS-5's single merged-UT rate,
  since that survey already reports them as one union territory.

(AI Kosh also lists an NFHS-5 factsheet dataset in our catalogue, but
it isn't in AI Kosh's bulk-downloadable set and its file-download
endpoint's required version parameter couldn't be determined without
guessing -- see `ingestion/nfhs5_tobacco.py`'s docstring. The DHS
Program PDF fetched here is the actual primary source either way.)

## reference_constants.json

| Key | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `national_oral_cancer_incidence_per_100k` | 10.4 | National Cancer Registry Programme (NCRP), India -- age-standardized rate |
| `national_tobacco_use_men_pct` / `_women_pct` | 38.0 / 8.9 | NFHS-5 Table 2.36, national total row |
| `sweden_benchmark_reduction_pct` | 44 | Sweden's documented reduction in tobacco-related mortality vs. the EU average, achieved via smoking-to-snus substitution (smoking prevalence fell from ~35%/28% in 1980 to 5.8% by 2022) |
| `female_share_of_population` | 940/1940 | 2011 Census national sex ratio (~940 females per 1000 males) |
| `girls_9_14_share_of_population` | 0.058 | Estimated from 2011 Census age structure (~29% of population aged 0-14, evenly split across ages and sex) -- an approximation, not a measured figure |
| `cervical_cancer_incidence_per_100k_women` | 18 | GLOBOCAN 2020, India's national age-standardized incidence rate |
| `hpv_vaccine_efficacy` | 0.90 | Conservative figure within published clinical trial ranges for HPV vaccines |

**Confidence note:** `nfhs5_tobacco_use_by_state.csv` and
`state_population_2011.csv` were read directly from primary source
documents. The NCRP and Sweden figures in `reference_constants.json`
were obtained via search results that cite those primary sources,
not by opening and reading the primary NCRP/Sweden documents
directly -- a real but slightly lower confidence level than the
tobacco/population tables.

**Important scientific caveat on the Sweden figure:** published
epidemiological literature shows snus use has a *null* association
with oral cancer specifically (pooled prospective studies, relative
risk ~0.86-1.1). Sweden's 44% figure reflects overall
tobacco-related mortality reduction (dominated by lung cancer and
other smoking-attributable disease via smoking cessation), not a
measured oral-cancer-specific effect. It is used in the oral cancer
simulation as a policy-ambition benchmark for a comparable reduction
in India's own tobacco use, not as a claim that snus itself lowers
oral cancer risk.
